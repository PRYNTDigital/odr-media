02 What's broken · Website

The site sells homes. It doesn't sell Old Dominion.

Brand

Your email is "lofty@"

Every page shows lofty@odrva.com in the header and "Powered by Lofty Inc." in the footer. Visitors see your software vendor's name before they see an agent's.

Offices

Five offices, no office pages

The Contact page lists all five offices, one line each, and that's it. There are no office pages, so none of your Google profiles has anywhere specific to send people.

Team

Two team pages, one with strangers on top

/agent lists your agents. /agents is the template's version, topped with a stock photo of a group who don't work for you, and it's where the Sell page's "Talk to an Agent" button goes.

Search

Google can't tell the pages apart

Pages share the title "… Old Dominion Realty - Old Dominion Realty", the Sell page uses the home-search description, and the homepage has no main heading.

Content

85 blog posts nobody wrote

No dates, no authors, and near-copies like two different "Curb Appeal" posts for Harrisonburg. Google treats templated posts like this as thin.

Details

Small things a seller notices

The homepage says 65 years and the team page says 60+ (you're at 64). A commercial listing on the homepage shows a $1 price. The Sell page photo is a stock family.

02 What's broken · Tracking, ads and brands

Nobody can say which marketing brought in a client.

Tracking

Three analytics IDs, two Meta pixels

The homepage loads three Google Analytics IDs and a Tag Manager container, so traffic gets split or counted twice. Two Meta pixels load too, and from the outside we can't tell whether either one belongs to your ad account.

Ads

Boosts, not campaigns

Your four current ads are graphics posted one at a time and sent to the homepage. The oldest has fewer than 100 impressions after seven weeks. One runs under a page called "Virginia Real Estate Service" instead of your name.

Brands

Three web addresses, two brands

odrnow.com for search, odrva.com for email, olddominionrealty.com forwarding, and odrpm.com for property management with no link back. A customer can't tell they're one company.

Sellers

Nothing for sellers to grab

The home-value tool works, but there's no local proof next to it: no sold numbers, no days on market, no reviews. It's the same page every brokerage on this software has.

Recruiting

No page for agents

There's no "join us" page or ad, even though agent stories are your best content and agents are how a brokerage grows.

Rankings

Missing from the searches that pay

Your name ranks fine. Searches like "homes for sale harrisonburg va" go to Zillow, Kline May and Funkhouser.

03 The fix · Search, domains and cleanup

The rest of what's broken, and what we'd do about each one.

Rankings

A page for every town you sell in

To show up for "homes for sale harrisonburg va" and the same search in your other towns, each town gets its own page on the new site. It has local copy written with your agents, that town's current listings from your home search, your sold numbers there, and the agents who work it. Each page is built to rank in Google for "homes for sale" in that town.

Blog

Cut the 85 templated posts down

We check which posts get any traffic. The near-copies and the ones nobody reads come down, and each one forwards to the matching town page so nothing that ranks is lost. After that, one post a month goes up, written with an agent, about one town and its real market numbers.

Domains

One address for the company

olddominionrealty.com is the company site. odrnow.com stays as the home-search address, linked as "Search homes" from every page, so agents' existing links keep working. odrva.com is only for email. Every old page that moves forwards to its new one.

Property management

Every complaint seen and answered

Each property management review gets a reply within two business days. Bad ones go straight to your property manager with a follow-up date, and once the problem is fixed we ask the tenant to update their review. Owners and tenants who renew get asked for a review too. The repairs are on your team. We make sure none of them get missed.

03 The fix · Ads and tracking

Measure first, then run two Meta campaigns that don't stop.

Tracking first

One Google Analytics property, one Meta pixel on your own ad account with server-side tracking, and every form and call tagged with where it came from. Leads keep going to the same place they go now, now labeled with their source.

Then the ads

Nothing goes live until the tracking is in, so the first month of spend is measurable.

  1. Seller leads. Facebook and Instagram ads to homeowners in each office's area, sending them to the home-value page with your local numbers on it.
  2. New listings, on video. Every new listing runs as a short video ad made from Great Valley Digital's footage, to people who've visited your site and people near the property. No more template graphics or one-off boosts.

Ad spend is billed by Meta directly and the ad account stays in Old Dominion's name.
